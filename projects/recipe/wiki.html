History

One of the first mentions of pancakes being eaten for pleasure occurs in 500 BCE by the Greek physician Galen. These pancakes, called Tiganites, directly translate to “frying pan,” which makes sense because they require being fried in an abundant amount of oil.

Pancakes as we know them began to take shape via the Dutch. The earliest settlers to the United States came from the Netherlands, and with them came pancake culture. Known as pannenkoeken, they had all the makings of what we consider pancakes today, except for one key factor: leavening agents had not yet been discovered.

The invention of baking powder in 1859 gave rise (no pun intended) to the pancakes we know today.


Regions

Pancakes are made all over the world, and different regions have their own versions. Some are thin and crispy, while others are thick, fluffy, savory, or made with different grains.

Pancakes around the world
  • Greece - Tiganites
  • England / Western Europe - Shrove Tuesday pancakes
  • Netherlands - Pannenkoeken
  • United States - Johnnycakes and fluffy pancakes
  • Ethiopia - Injera
  • Russia - Blini
  • Italy - Farinata
  • Vietnam - Bánh xèo
  • China - Bing
  • India - Dosa and Roti

Variations

  1. Thickness and texture: American-style pancakes, like the IHOP copycat recipe, use baking powder and baking soda to make them thick and fluffy, while English pancakes and crêpes are much thinner.
  2. Toppings and fillings: Pancakes can be served simply with butter and syrup, or filled and topped with things like jam, cream cheese, chocolate, fruit, or savory ingredients.
  3. Sweet vs. savory: In some places pancakes are mainly a sweet breakfast food, while other cultures make savory versions, such as Japanese okonomiyaki or Korean pancakes.
